Australia's Favorite Pets

Popularity, trends, preferences and demographics
of Australian companion animals.

Section 1: Introduction

Pets play an essential role in Australian society and culture, with ownership reaching massive heights across households nationwide.

Nearly 73% of Australian households share their homes with at least one companion animal, indicating that pets are essential members of domestic life.

Section 2: Ownership Trends Over Time

Tracking how ownership and spending have changed.
Data comes from recent national surveys.

Figure 01 · Line chart

Pet Ownership Surged During the Pandemic and Kept Rising

Share of Australian households owning each kind of pet, by survey year

Ownership rose after 2019

From 61% of households in 2019 to 73% in 2025.

The pandemic drove most of it

Two thirds of the rise came by 2022.

Dogs lead cats

In 2025, dogs reached 49% and cats 34%.

Figure 02 · Dumbbell chart

Spending on Pets Is Up About 35% in Three Years, Led by Dogs

Estimated annual spending by pet type, $ billions. Grey dot = 2022, dark dot = 2025. Labels show the 2025 value and the change since 2022. AMA reports 35% for all pets.

Spending is up 35%

From $15.7 billion in 2022 to $21.3 billion in 2025.

Dogs drive the rise

Dogs account for $13.2 billion, up 49%.

Cats grew more slowly

Cat spending rose 22%, to $5.9 billion.

Section 5: Conclusion

Summarizing the deep emotional bonds Australians share with their companion animals against modern economic realities.

Although pet ownership remains strong, rising financial costs have become the main reason households hesitate about getting another pet.
