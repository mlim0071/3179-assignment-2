Australia's Favorite Pets

Investigating the popularity, trends, preferences, and demographics of companion animals across Australia.

Section 1: Introduction

Pets play an essential role in Australian society and culture, with ownership reaching massive heights across households nationwide.

Key Takeaway: Nearly 73% of Australian households share their homes with at least one companion animal, highlighting that pets are essential members of domestic life.

Section 2: Ownership Trends Over Time

Tracking how pet ownership and national spending on pets have changed across recent national surveys.

Ownership growth: The share of households owning a pet rose from 61% in 2019 to 73% in 2025, with most of the increase during the pandemic years.
Spending growth: Estimated spending rose 35%, from $15.7 billion in 2022 (recalculated by AMA) to $21.3 billion in 2025. Dogs account for $13.2 billion of it and cats $5.9 billion, and dog spending grew the most (+49%).

Section 3: Household Preferences

Examining which species dominate Australian households and comparing total animal populations to owning homes.

Penetration Leaderboard: Dogs are shown as Australia's top companion animal, found in nearly 50% of all households, followed closely by cats.
Household Ownership: Isolating owning households onto a Cleveland dot plot displays exact household numbers clearly without compression from large pet populations.
Population Ranking: Comparing total animal populations across a common baseline clearly highlights the massive volume of fish and mammalian pets without scale distortion.

Section 4: Geographic Distribution & Demographics

Comparing how RSPCA shelters in each state and territory take in and rehome animals, and which Australians own pets.

Share rehomed: The ACT (69%), NT (66%) and Tasmania (66%) rehome the biggest share of the animals they receive, while Western Australia (50%) and NSW (52%) rehome the smallest. Their figures exclude pound animals, so compare them with care.
Absolute volume: Queensland (14,420) and NSW (9,941) receive the most animals in RSPCA shelters, but counts mostly follow population, so the next map adjusts for it. ACT, NT and Tasmania figures exclude pound animals.
Intake per person: Circle size shows population and colour shows animals received per 100,000 people. The ACT (522) takes in nearly twice as many as any other state, followed by SA (280) and QLD (254).
Who owns pets: Dog owners are 8.7 points more likely than non-owners to own their home, while cat owners barely differ (−1.1). Owners of any pet are also less likely to be single, separated, divorced or widowed.

Section 5: Conclusion

Summarizing the deep emotional bonds Australians share with their companion animals against modern economic realities.

The 2022–2025 Shift: Although pet ownership remains strong, rising financial costs have become the main reason households hesitate about getting another pet.
