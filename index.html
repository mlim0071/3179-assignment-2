Australia's Favorite Pets

Investigating the popularity, trends, preferences, and demographics of companion animals across Australia.

Section 1: Introduction

Pets play an essential role in Australian society and culture, with ownership reaching massive heights across households nationwide.

Key Takeaway: Nearly 73% of Australian households share their homes with at least one companion animal, highlighting that pets are essential members of domestic life.

Section 2: Ownership Trends Over Time

Tracking how total pet populations and national expenditure have evolved across recent multi-year national surveys.

Population Growth: Both cat and dog populations have steadily increased as animal companionship was desired by more households.
Normalized Expenditure Composition: Using a 100% normalized area chart removes baseline distortion, enabling accurate comparisons of how spending priorities (such as veterinary and food services) have shifted over time.

Section 3: Household Preferences

Examining which species dominate Australian households and comparing total animal populations to owning homes.

Penetration Leaderboard: Dogs are shown as Australia's top companion animal, found in nearly 50% of all households, followed closely by cats.
Household Ownership: Isolating owning households onto a Cleveland dot plot displays exact household numbers clearly without compression from large pet populations.
Population Ranking: Comparing total animal populations across a common baseline clearly highlights the massive volume of fish and mammalian pets without scale distortion.

Section 4: Geographic Distribution & Demographics

Exploring state-level differences in pet ownership density across Australian states and territories.

Regional Variance: Regional and suburban states record higher pet density percentages compared to densely populated metropolitan capitals.

Section 5: Conclusion

Summarizing the deep emotional bonds Australians share with their companion animals against modern economic realities.

The 2022–2025 Shift: Although pet ownership remains strong, rising financial costs have become the main reason households hesitate about getting another pet.
